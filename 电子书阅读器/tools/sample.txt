The Lantern Keeper

Chapter One - The Small Machine

The workshop smelled of solder and old paper, and on the bench
stood a board no bigger than a matchbox. On its little screen a
single line of text waited, patient as a patient thing.

"Read me," it seemed to say. "I have room for five lines and no
more, so choose your words carefully."

Mara laughed at that. She had built the thing from parts her
father had left in a tin: a chip with too few pins, a flash memory
that held eight million bytes, and a display that glowed the exact
blue of a winter evening.

She typed a sentence and watched it appear, letter by letter, each
one six pixels wide and eleven tall. Then she typed another, and
another, until the screen was full.

Chapter Two - What Fits

A screen this small teaches you something about stories. You
cannot waste a line. Every word has to earn its place, and when
the page is full you press a button and the words slide away,
making room for the next ones.

"It's like breathing," she told the cat. "In, and out."

The cat did not answer. The cat was busy sitting on the flash
memory, which is what cats do with important things.

So she kept going, page after page, through the quiet afternoon,
until the whole book fit inside a chip the size of a fingernail.

THE END
